## 1. Positioning hierarchy

From most compressed to most expansive. Each level is a faithful expansion of the
one above it — they must not drift apart in meaning.

| Level | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Core position | 5 words | Internal north star; wordmark tagline candidate |
| Short descriptor | ~10 words | Nav, meta description, "what is this" one-liner |
| One-sentence explanation | 1 sentence | Homepage subhead, sales intro, docs intro |
| 30-second explanation | 1 short paragraph | First sales call, "tell me more," about page opener |
| Operator-focused explanation | 1 paragraph | Operator landing page, onboarding, operator pitch |
| Facility-facing explanation | 1 paragraph | Only if/when Nemryn markets to facilities directly |
| Internal product thesis | 1–2 sentences | Roadmap and design decisions; not customer-facing |

## 2. Core position

> **Operating infrastructure for medical transportation.**

- Five words. Not a slogan — a category claim.
- "Infrastructure" is deliberate: it says foundational, dependable, always-on,
  the thing the business runs *on*.
- Do not soften to "platform," "solution," or "software suite" at this level.

## 3. Short descriptor

> **Software that runs the day-to-day operation of an NEMT business.**

Alternatives (same meaning, different emphasis — choose per context, do not
invent new ones):

- *The system NEMT operators run their service on.*
- *Operating infrastructure for non-emergency medical transportation.*

## 4. One-sentence explanation

> **Nemryn is the system an NEMT operator uses to see the true state of their
> service, know what needs a decision next, and prove what happened — from one
> vehicle to a large fleet.**

Notes for whoever edits this later:

- Keep the three verbs: **see** (state), **decide** (what's next), **prove**
  (what happened). They map to the product thesis.
- Keep "from one vehicle to a large fleet" — the maturity range is a
  differentiator, not a footnote.
- Do not add "AI," "smart," "optimized," "seamless," or "all-in-one."

## 5. 30-second explanation

> An NEMT operation runs on trips that can't be missed — dialysis, discharges,
> chemotherapy — but most operators coordinate them with a stack of spreadsheets,
> phone calls, and portals that don't talk to each other. Nemryn replaces that
> with one operating system for the whole operation: requests come in and become
> scheduled trips, every trip has an owner and a readiness state, problems
> surface before they fail instead of after, and a completed trip is a proven,
> billable trip by default. It works the same way for a one-vehicle operator and
> a fleet — it just shows more as the operation grows.

## 6. Operator-focused explanation

> You already know what's happening in your operation — it's in your head, your
> notebook, and three group texts. Nemryn puts it in one place. You open the
> Operations Brief and see today at a glance: what's running, what's ready for
> tomorrow, and the short list of things that need you right now. Every trip
> shows who's responsible and whether it's actually ready — the right vehicle,
> a confirmed driver, a valid authorization. When something slips, you see it
> while there's still time to fix it. And when the day is done, the record of
> what you delivered is complete enough to bill and to show a facility. No new
> dispatch department required — Nemryn fits the operation you have now and grows
> with the one you're building.

## 7. Facility-facing explanation (conditional — only if Nemryn markets to facilities)

> When your transportation provider runs on Nemryn, your standing orders and
> one-off requests land in a single place, you can see whether a trip is
> confirmed without making a call, and every completed trip comes back with a
> clean record of pickup and drop-off. Fewer missed appointments, fewer status
> calls, and proof of service you can file.

Do not develop this further in G1 — facility marketing is not a decided
go-to-market motion.

## 8. Internal product thesis (not customer-facing)

> An NEMT operation should run on a system that continuously reports the true
> state of service and the next decision required, and that captures what was
> promised and what happened well enough to prove it and get paid. Nemryn is that
> system; the trip is its primary object; readiness and exceptions are its
> organizing principle, not the calendar.

## 9. Positioning guardrails

### Words to avoid in positioning and marketing copy

Unless in a direct quotation or a historical note:

`smart` · `intelligent` · `AI` / `AI-powered` · `optimization` / `optimized` ·
`revolutionary` · `seamless` · `all-in-one` · `powerful` · `transform` /
`transformative` · `next-generation` · `cutting-edge` · `innovative` ·
`game-changing` · `effortless` · `magic` / `magical` · `supercharge` ·
`reimagine` · seamlessly-anything · `end-to-end` (as a filler adjective;
acceptable only when literally describing trip lifecycle coverage)

### Words to prefer (the operational register)

`requests` · `trips` · `readiness` · `assignments` · `exceptions` · `service` ·
`capacity` · `coordination` · `responsibility` · `attention` · `proof` ·
`revenue` · `confirmed` · `ready` · `owner` · `on time` · `recovery` ·
`standing order` · `recurring service` · `manifest` · `dispatch`

### Structural rules

- **Claim capability, not adjectives.** "Every trip shows who's responsible" beats
  "powerful accountability features."
- **No manufactured urgency.** Nemryn reduces stress; marketing must not
  generate it.
- **No fear-selling of patient harm.** The stakes are real and can be stated
  plainly ("a missed trip is a missed dialysis appointment"). Do not dramatize
  or use frightened-patient imagery.
- **Name the maturity range early.** "From one vehicle to a large fleet" is part
  of the pitch, not fine print.
- **Never imply Nemryn is a healthcare provider, a payer, or a broker.**
- **Never co-brand with Zenward Mobility.** If Zenward Mobility appears in
  marketing it is as a named customer, clearly labelled, with permission.

## 10. Competitive framing (internal)

- **Versus spreadsheets + phone + portals (the real incumbent):** Nemryn is one
  system instead of five disconnected ones; it makes state visible and problems
  early.
- **Versus generic dispatch / field-service software:** Nemryn is built for the
  medical-transportation trip — authorizations, mobility needs, standing orders,
  proof of service — not a generic job with a healthcare skin.
- **Versus brokerage platforms:** Nemryn is the operator's own system, not a
  marketplace that sits between the operator and the payer.
- **Versus other NEMT software (e.g. hibambi.com and peers):** do not position by
  feature-comparison against them and do not imitate them. Position on the
  operating-infrastructure claim and the maturity range. See the Competitor Rule
  in [`04-visual-direction.md`](./04-visual-direction.md).
